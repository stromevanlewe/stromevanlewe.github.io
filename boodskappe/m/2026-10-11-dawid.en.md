# Beholding (part 1)

*Dawid & Annemarie de Greeff · 11 October 2026*

What does it take to be built into God's house? It starts with hunger. *"Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they shall be satisfied"* (Matthew 5:6).

Peter describes us as stones that God is building into a spiritual house: *"As you come to him, a living stone… you yourselves like living stones are being built up as a spiritual house"* (1 Peter 2:4–5). Notice when the building happens: *as we come to Him*. Coming to Jesus and looking at Him is how we are built in.

We are the wall. We are the stones. So the question for this message is simple: **how does a dull stone become a shining stone in God's wall?**

## One. God calls first

> *Beholding does not start with us. God comes looking, God points to Jesus, and God looks at us before we ever look at Him.*

### A pebble from the Orange River

![Pebbles on the bank of the Orange River at sunset](../artikels/img/aanskou/orange-river-pebbles.jpg "The banks of the Orange River, near Hopetown")

In 1867, near Hopetown on the banks of the Orange River, fifteen-year-old Erasmus Jacobs picked up a pebble. Nobody knew what it was. It was posted off in an ordinary envelope to be checked. It turned out to be a diamond, the first ever found in South Africa: the Eureka Diamond, which today lies in the museum in Kimberley.

There are many kinds of stones. Some are dull, and some are diamonds. But even a diamond has no light of its own. In a dark room it is dark. It only shines when light goes in and comes back out.

That is us on our own: dull, not built in, with no light of our own. From the beginning, God's design was this: we turn to Him, to His Son, and we shine.

> *"And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another."* (2 Corinthians 3:18)

As we look at Jesus, God shapes us to look like Him.

### God comes looking

It starts with God. In the garden, Adam and Eve hide, and God comes looking: *"Where are you?"* (Genesis 3:9). He comes for all of us, every one of us undeserving and every one of us loved: *"But God, being rich in mercy… even when we were dead in our trespasses, made us alive together with Christ"* (Ephesians 2:4–5).

And all through history He keeps pointing us to Jesus. The Bible has a small word we easily skip over: *behold*. In Hebrew it is *hinneh*, and it means: "Look! See this! Don't miss it!" The first *behold* in this story isn't ours. It is God's. He points us to Jesus.

### The cure was a look

![A bronze serpent on a pole in the wilderness](../artikels/img/aanskou/bronze-serpent.jpg "The cure was not a task. It was a look.")

In the desert, Israel was bitten by snakes. God told Moses to put a bronze snake on a pole, and whoever was bitten and *looked* at it lived (Numbers 21:8–9). Jesus said that was a picture of Himself on the cross (John 3:14–15). **The cure wasn't a task. It was a look.**

Then John the Baptist saw Jesus coming, and he pointed:

>> “Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!”
>> — John 1:29

On a snowy Sunday in 1850, a fifteen-year-old called Charles Spurgeon sat in a tiny chapel. The preacher could hardly preach. He just kept calling out one verse: *"Look unto me, and be ye saved, all the ends of the earth"* (Isaiah 45:22, KJV). "Look! Look!" The boy looked, and became one of the greatest preachers who ever lived. And today God still says to us: *Look at Me.*

>> He came looking for us before we ever looked for Him.

**In a nutshell:** on our own we are dull stones with no light of our own. But God invites us: "Behold! The Lamb of God." He came looking for us before we ever looked for Him. The cure is not a task. It is a look.

## Two. What it means to behold

> *To behold is more than a glance. It is when your eyes and your heart connect, and you actually see Him. And whatever we keep looking at shapes us.*

### Glance, gaze, behold

The Bible has different words for seeing. In Hebrew the plain word for seeing, or glancing, is *ra'ah*. But there is a deeper word: *chazah*. It means to gaze: to fix your eyes on something and drink it in, until it changes you.

![A rainbow in the spray of the Vanderkloof Dam](../artikels/img/aanskou/vanderkloof-rainbow.jpg "The Vanderkloof Dam: “His voice was like the roar of many waters”")

When Annemarie's dad was ill in hospital in Bloemfontein, she drove between Kimberley and Bloemfontein every day, and saw the most spectacular sunsets she had ever seen. All she wanted to do was stare at those colours. At the beach in the late afternoon she can sit and watch the waves roll in, time after time. And at the Vanderkloof Dam, with the water rushing down, she just wanted to stand there and take in that enormous amount of water and its thundering sound. John describes Jesus like that: *"His voice was like the roar of many waters"* (Revelation 1:15). And there was a rainbow in the spray. When Ezekiel saw God's glory, that is what it looked like: *"Like the appearance of the bow that is in the cloud on the day of rain… Such was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD"* (Ezekiel 1:28).

That is what it means to gaze. You give your full attention, and you don't want anything to keep you from taking in the moment.

But there is one more step.

>> Gazing is keeping your eyes on something. Beholding is when your eyes and your heart connect, and you actually see Him.

You can stare at a page of your Bible for ten minutes and see nothing. Or one line can stop you, because suddenly you see Jesus in it. Even that seeing is a gift we ask for: *"Open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of your law"* (Psalm 119:18).

### What are you beholding?

Think about what you are doing when you say: "Just wait a minute. Not now. Let me just finish this. Shhh!" We stare at many things every day: our phones, our series, YouTube, other people, dirty kitchens. And sometimes we stare at nothing at all, while our thoughts churn over worries, fears, unfairness and envy.

David was a king with a hundred and one things on his mind: decisions, people to lead, wars to fight. And yet he wrote:

> *"One thing have I asked of the LORD, that will I seek after: that I may dwell in the house of the LORD all the days of my life, to gaze upon the beauty of the LORD."* (Psalm 27:4)

The word for beauty there is *no'am*: delightfulness, loveliness, the kind of beauty that makes you linger because you can't look away. Not many things. One thing: a Person worth gazing at forever.

### We become what we behold

Why does this matter? **Because we become what we behold.** Think of teenagers and their heroes: a music star, an actor, a rugby player. They watch every move and copy the hairstyle, the clothes and the way of talking. Nobody tells them to; admiration does it. John Piper says it simply: "You become most like what you admire most."

So the question is not *if* you are being shaped. You are being shaped every day. The question is: **by what?** Maybe you have tried hard. You deleted apps, you open the Bible app first, you pray, read, give and do good, and still you see nothing change. That is because change begins with *sight*, not *effort*. You cannot work yourself into being changed. You need to look at Jesus until His beauty remakes you.

>> Change begins with sight, not effort.

## Three. Jesus, the One worth beholding

> *If we become what we behold, everything depends on what God is really like. And we see that in Jesus: the King who came down.*

### Whoever has seen Me has seen the Father

No one can look straight at God. *"No one has ever seen God. But the unique One, who is himself God, is near to the Father's heart. He has revealed God to us"* (John 1:18, NLT). Jesus is God's explanation of Himself. One evening Philip asked Him to show them the Father, and Jesus answered:

> *"Have I been with you so long, and you still do not know me, Philip? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father."* (John 14:9)

Hebrews calls Him *"the radiance of the glory of God and the exact imprint of his nature"* (Hebrews 1:3). Radiance is light shining out of its source, like sunlight from the sun. An exact imprint is like a stamp pressed into wax: every line of the stamp shows. Everything God is, we see in Jesus.

### A star that turned away

To see how bright Jesus is, it helps to look for a moment at the opposite. Many Bible teachers through the ages have seen, behind the proud kings of Ezekiel 28 and Isaiah 14, the first rebel: the one we know as Lucifer, "light-bearer". He was made to shine, *"perfect in beauty"*, covered in precious stones (Ezekiel 28:12–13). But a gem has no light of its own. His light was borrowed.

Then: *"Your heart was proud because of your beauty"* (Ezekiel 28:17). He stopped looking at the light and started looking at himself. *"I will ascend… I will set my throne on high… I will make myself like the Most High"* (Isaiah 14:13–14). I will. I will. I will. A gem that turns away from the light goes dark. Pride always goes up, and it always says: *notice me*.

### Now look at Jesus

Jesus does not have borrowed light. He is God. All the glory is His. And what did He do with it?

> *"Though he was in the form of God, [he] did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant… he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross."* (Philippians 2:6–8)

**Lucifer's light was borrowed, and he tried to keep it for himself. Jesus' light was His own, and He gave it away. Lucifer went up. Jesus came down.** Andrew Selley put it like this: "Lucifer's beauty went to his head. Jesus' beauty went to a cross." That is the beauty of Jesus.

>> Lucifer went up. Jesus came down.

Take a moment to behold it:

- **What He lives for.** Not His own glory: *"I glorified you on earth, having accomplished the work that you gave me to do"* (John 17:4). He did *"only what he sees the Father doing"* (John 5:19). Jesus Himself lived by beholding.
- **His heart.** The only time He describes it, He says: *"I am gentle and lowly in heart"* (Matthew 11:29).
- **The night before the cross.** The King of kings gets up from the table, wraps a towel around His waist and washes His disciples' dirty feet, Judas' too (John 13:4–5).
- **Gethsemane.** Lucifer said, "I will." Jesus says, *"Not my will, but yours, be done"* (Luke 22:42).
- **The cross.** *"He had no form or majesty that we should look at him"* (Isaiah 53:2). The most beautiful One was disfigured, for us.
- **Resurrection morning.** He has just defeated death, and Mary thinks He is the gardener (John 20:15). He doesn't need to be noticed.

![A wooden cross on a hill at sunrise](../artikels/img/aanskou/cross.jpg "“Lucifer's beauty went to his head. Jesus' beauty went to a cross.”")

That is what God is like. Satan says: "Notice me." Jesus says: "Notice the Father." The Holy Spirit says: "Look at Jesus" (John 16:14). The whole of God points away from Himself. Humility isn't listed as a fruit of the Spirit, but it is the soil all the fruit grows in. As we behold His humility, it grows in us.

So here is a question to carry into every week, in every situation, at work, at home and in every conversation: **Is this going up, or going down?** Up is Lucifer's way. Down is Jesus' way.

### Living stones

A stone can't see anything. That is how the Bible describes a heart without God. But God promises: *"I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh. And I will put my Spirit within you"* (Ezekiel 36:26–27). That is why Peter calls us *living* stones. God makes dead stones alive, and a living heart can see.

And then the change begins: we *"are being transformed into the same image"* (2 Corinthians 3:18). We don't transform ourselves. It is done *to* us, by the Spirit, and it keeps happening. It is the same word used when Jesus was transfigured on the mountain and *"his face shone like the sun"* (Matthew 17:2). As Paul Washer says: "We grow in conformity to Christ and in devotion to Christ by seeing more and more of Christ." Not a better version of ourselves, but more like Jesus, slowly, *"from one degree of glory to another"*.

## Four. How we keep looking

> *These things don't change us. Only Jesus changes us. They simply put us where He can change us.*

First, something important: what follows are not four new rules. And remember: before we ever looked at Him, He looked at us. *"And Jesus, looking at him, loved him"* (Mark 10:21).

### 1. We come hungry

People can hear the same Word and walk away differently. The difference isn't access. It is appetite. *"As a deer pants for flowing streams, so pants my soul for you, O God"* (Psalm 42:1).

![Blind Bartimaeus calling out by the roadside with his arms raised](../artikels/img/aanskou/bartimaeus.jpg "Bartimaeus kept shouting for Jesus (Mark 10:46–52)")

Blind Bartimaeus kept shouting for Jesus, even when people told him to be quiet (Mark 10:46–52). The woman who had been bleeding for twelve years pressed through the crowd just to touch His clothes (Mark 5:25–34).

![A hand reaching through the crowd for the tassel of a cloak](../artikels/img/aanskou/hem.jpg "She pressed through the crowd just to touch His clothes (Mark 5:27–28)")

Think of an ice cube. In the freezer it stays hard for weeks. Bring it into the sun, and in minutes it melts. Our hearts can be like that: cold and hard. Hunger is what brings us out of the freezer and into the warmth of His presence, and there the hard heart begins to melt. So ask yourself: *do I long to be changed?*

### 2. We fix our eyes on what is true

The blessed person in Psalm 1 doesn't just glance at God's Word now and then: *"his delight is in the law of the LORD, and on his law he meditates day and night"* (Psalm 1:2). And we carry the Word with us, like Mary, who *"treasured up all these things, pondering them in her heart"* (Luke 2:19). When you read, ask: *What does this show me about Jesus?*

### 3. We take our thoughts captive

The battle is fought at the level of our attention. When a thought doesn't line up with God's Word, we don't have to keep replaying it. We *"take every thought captive to obey Christ"* (2 Corinthians 10:5) and let God transform us *"by the renewal of your mind"* (Romans 12:2). Distraction redirects your gaze, and it is dangerous because it never stops. You may not be fighting God at all. You may just be distracted, your attention in too many pieces for the truth to settle.

### 4. We stay long enough

We become what we keep coming back to, not what we hear once. *"Whatever is true, whatever is honourable, whatever is just, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely… think about these things"* (Philippians 4:8). James talks about someone who looks in a mirror, walks away and immediately forgets what he looks like (James 1:23–24). His problem wasn't that he didn't see. He just didn't stay long enough.

## Keep looking at Jesus

So let's go back to the dull stone in the riverbed. God doesn't walk past it. He calls: "Behold!" He takes hearts as hard as stone and softens them by His Spirit, so that we can see. And He shows us His face in Jesus: the King who came down, who washed feet, who said "not my will", and who gave His beauty away on a cross. As we keep looking at Him, something happens that we could never do ourselves. The stone begins to shine, with a light that isn't its own.

Beholding Jesus starts now, but it never ends. It ends where David longed to be: *"They will see his face"* (Revelation 22:4). Until then, don't just glance. Gaze, and behold.

>> Keep looking at Jesus. We become what we behold.

Maybe you have never really looked at Jesus. You have heard about Him, perhaps glanced at Him, but never turned and looked. Today He says to you: *"Turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth!"* (Isaiah 45:22). The cure is still a look. And He has already looked at you. You don't have to earn His attention. You only have to turn.
