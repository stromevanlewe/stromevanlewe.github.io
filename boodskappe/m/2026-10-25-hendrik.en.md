# Don't Only Be Spectators

*Hendrik van der Merwe · 25 October 2026*

Yesterday I dropped Engela at the doctor's rooms — not because I needed to see the doctor, but because she works there. And while I was sitting there, a dog came running past. You could see at one glance that this dog was a long way from home.

It was running with its hazards on. No signal. The WiFi was off. It did not know which tree was which, it did not know where to leave its mark, and it did not know a single face. Other dogs came out and barked at it, and what they were saying was simple enough: *who are you, and you are not coming near what is mine.*

A dog at home is a different animal. It knows every person in that yard and it has a particular relationship with each one. It knows what belongs there and what does not. It knows the smells, the corners, the boundaries. And because it knows all of that, it can guard the place. Take the knowledge away and you have not taken away the dog's loyalty — you have taken away its ability to do anything with it.

That is what a church looks like when it is not well. Not a church without love, and not a church without effort. A church that has forgotten who it is in Christ, and so runs in circles, confused, with its hazards on.

So the question this morning is not whether we love the church. It is whether we know what it is.

## One. We *are* receiving

> *"Therefore, since we are receiving a kingdom which cannot be shaken, let us have grace, by which we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear. For our God is a consuming fire."*
> Hebrews 12:28-29

I want to pull this verse apart and take the building blocks out of it one at a time, because every phrase in it says something about what the church is.

Start with two words: *we are.*

Present continuous. Not *we have received*, past and finished. Not *we will receive*, somewhere off in the future. We **are** receiving — it is happening now, and it is still happening. We have not arrived.

That matters, because we like to sit where we are comfortable. Every one of us finds a seat and settles into it, and there is nothing wrong with that. But as we grow, we have to find the seat Jesus has for us, which is not always the one we chose.

Most of us have quietly reversed the order. We have decided what church is supposed to be for us — a place where my need is met, a place where people care about me. All of that is true, and none of it is first. We say to Jesus, without ever saying it out loud: I will come if the worship is right. I will come if it is done the way I know it.

A child does not do that. Think of what Jesus did when the argument started:

> *"At that time the disciples came to Jesus, saying, 'Who then is greatest in the kingdom of heaven?' Then Jesus called a little child to Him, set him in the midst of them, and said, 'Assuredly, I say to you, unless you are converted and become as little children, you will by no means enter the kingdom of heaven.'"*
> Matthew 18:1-3

A child comes and says: here I am. What is Your kingdom? What is Your church? Where do I fit?

Receiving a kingdom means something in God's life is going to change something in mine — His way of thinking, His way of doing. If I am receiving, I am being changed. If nothing is changing, I may be attending, but I am not receiving.

**In short:** We are still receiving, which means we have not arrived — and the seat we chose for ourselves is not always the one He has for us.

## Two. A kingdom that cannot be shaken

Look at what the verse promises: *a kingdom which cannot be shaken.*

God puts something in the middle of the church that does not move, so that you have a reference point. Something that cannot be altered, that can be trusted, that you can hold onto when everything else is sliding. Something that means you do not have to be afraid of what you might lose, or of what people might think of you.

And we need that, because people shake easily.

How quickly do we disappoint each other? Someone says something. Hendrik does something, or Piet does something, and we are so disappointed, and the next thought is: I am not coming to church.

Now — what is actually wrong there? Is it that we saw each other's faults? No. We will always see each other's faults. It is that we did not know what church is in the first place. We did not know what the Lord has for us, or what He is building in our lives, or how this faith is meant to be applied. So we were resting the whole thing on people, and people move.

Andrew puts it well: the church is a vehicle. It is a transport system for salvation. And in my own words, it is where steadfastness is found — where I am willingly part of something, where I cannot wait to be with my brother and my sister who are changing daily and growing daily just like me.

It is not a one-man event. It is a kingdom where Jesus is God. It is not a place where grown-ups argue about who is who and how it ought to be done and *I know how it was in my church and this is how I grew up.* It is a place where we learn to lay that down.

**In short:** The thing that cannot be shaken is not the people. Build on the people and you will be disappointed on schedule.

## Three. Let us have grace

Then the verse says: *let us have grace.* Some translations read this as ‘let us be thankful’.

Someone told me recently how hard it is to walk into a place where people do not know how to handle you. Where they are almost afraid of you. Keep that picture, because that must never be the church.

The church should be a place where I can explore. Where I can look at someone and think: the Lord wants to work in this person's life. He does not know why he is here. He thinks he needs this one thing, and that is why he came — but actually Jesus wants to become everything to him. So let me be interested in him. Let me find out how we connect.

That takes two sides. One of them has to be willing to say: I am letting go of the past, and I am letting God renew my thinking about what church is. I am opening myself to relationships, and to personalities that are not mine.

And that is not always easy. I know I do not have the easiest personality. It is not always easy for me either. But I know there is grace in this kingdom, because I watch it every day. I see it in my brother Dawid, who has been patient with me for a long time now. And in that patience there is something God is revealing in him — God gives him the love to love me.

That is how it starts for all of us. At first it is: this person is strange. But the more you grow in it, the more you lay yourself down, the more God hands you the life and the tools to love people.

It can be very ordinary. If you cannot find words, and someone needs a couch moved, and you hear about it, and you happen to have a vehicle — then you say, here I am. And do you know what happens next? You feel alive. Because you are finding out where you belong in the kingdom.

Grace is also what makes correction possible. And I say this with respect, and only as an example — a man like Frans can say: I already know what church is. I have been around, I know life. And if the Lord is working in that heart, the Lord can say — let Me show you what church is. Let Me show you what family is. The honest answer in that moment is not *I don't need to, I'm the leader.* If Jesus is not leading me, I have a problem, because then I cannot build anything. Everything built on the foundation of the Word has to be the truth. And I am only following the truth, the same as you.

**In short:** Grace is what makes the church a place where you can be known and corrected without losing your place.

## Four. Serving, and the fire

The next phrase: *by which we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear.*

We bring our offering. But remember Cain and Abel. One brought an offering and it was not accepted, and the other brought one and it was. And it is not simply that one was a nice piece of meat and the other was vegetables — I would also have taken the meat. It goes deeper than that. God looks into the heart. He knows why you want to build into the church. He knows when someone wants to build out of fear, and that will not build anything, it will break things down. So He wants to sort out the fear and turn it into faith in Him, until you are willing to say, like a child: *yes, Lord, take me.*

That is the happiness. And when someone comes into the kingdom and gives their heart to the Lord, what happens in heaven? Party time. And now that person can be built into the body of Christ.

Sometimes we come with a heart that does not really want to surrender — it is only testing. And then I do not feel at peace, because I do not know why I am in church. I have no position, and I feel uncomfortable. But once the Lord has worked those things out in the heart, and shown you who you are in Him, and shown you how to be pliable and how to put yourself in — then being in the body stops feeling like a demand and starts feeling like an honour. It stops being about human standards and positions, the thing those disciples were arguing about. It becomes: I have the heart of the Lord and I can do nothing else, because I am sold out to Him.

And then comes the last phrase: *for our God is a consuming fire.*

I have sat with that verse for years. And I have always explained it with an English proverb: *fire is a good servant but a bad master.* Fire cooks your food; fire in the veld will take your life.

This morning, at the prison, the Lord turned the whole thing around on me. I have been explaining it wrong.

>> God is a good master. But He is not your servant. We are His servants.

And what is the good of serving Him? He sets you free. Free from the one who comes to steal, kill and destroy — and He gives life, and gives it more abundantly (John 10:10). That is not about winning your old life back. It is not about recovering your old dreams. It is about discovering a whole new life, in Christ and in the church, and being free to be in the service of it.

**In short:** He is not a resource we manage. He is the Master — and serving Him is where the freedom is.

## Don't only be spectators

I want to close with Acts 17.

> *"And He has made from one blood every nation of men to dwell on all the face of the earth, and has determined their pre-appointed times and the boundaries of their dwellings, so that they should seek the Lord, in the hope that they might grope for Him and find Him, though He is not far from each one of us."*
> Acts 17:26-27

Look at how much division there is in the world, and how much pain. Humanity moved away from God through sin — through one man, Adam, and lying and stealing and hatred came in and changed people's hearts.

And God's way of bringing us back was also through one man. Jesus. He is the head and we are the body; we are His, we are joined to Him. So we are not *trying* to be the church, the way Adam's humanity tried in the flesh. We are the church as we surrender to God, as we are born into Him. And that means receiving His heart.

Now notice what the verse says God determined: their times, and **the boundaries of their dwelling**. Think about the dog again. Without knowing who belongs where, and what is what, it can guard nothing. That is exactly why it is so confused. And sometimes we sit in a church where people do not know who they are either, and do not know where to build themselves in, because each one is carrying a longing and a loss that has grown into thoughts of being lost.

So the Lord does not always start with the healing you came for. He starts by teaching you who you are in Him. That is why, when the disciples were arguing about who was the greatest, He knew: you are not building on the foundation. What I am about to give you, you will not even be able to receive unless you believe like children. You will be able to say My words. You will be able to tell people how to live rightly. But you will not be able to do it yourselves.

Do you know who the Lord can use? The one who does not know anything. You are qualified by not knowing.

One more thing, and then I am done. Sometimes we do not get answers from the Lord because we are not asking the right questions. *Lord, why is life so hard for me?* — rather than *Lord, what am I doing wrong that You want to change?* *Lord, why did people hurt me so badly?* — rather than *why am I still identifying myself by the hurt?* Think what a difference that one change would make.

We are so excited to build. And sometimes we ask: why don't the people come? But we could also turn and ask the other question. *Lord, are we a reflection of what Your church should be? When people come in, do they find answers? Do they find what they are looking for?*

I am not going to stand here and say I have prayed, so now we know everything. It is a journey. But I want to invite all of us into it. I want us to become streams of life — inviting people, taking them by the hand, and saying: build yourself in.

>> Don't only be spectators. Be the church of Jesus. Be His smile. Be His love.
