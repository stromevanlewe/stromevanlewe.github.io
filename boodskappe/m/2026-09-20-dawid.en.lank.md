# Offence: God's Invitation to Change

*A message by Dawid de Greeff, with contributions from Hendrik van der Merwe — 20 September 2026*

---

## The main idea

**God calls us to change — into the image of Jesus, into His righteousness. And He does not only call: He provides the ability.**

Offence often shows us exactly where that call has arrived. It flares up where there is a shortage of truth, where we resist change, or where we are leaning on our own ability.

**We do not bring our own clothes to His feast. Our part is to put on what He gives.**

---

## Where it starts

Someone says something — and something in you tightens.

You don't argue. You probably say nothing. But something in you has pulled back.

When last did that happen to you? Maybe something you heard in church, in a small group, or in a conversation afterwards irritated or upset you. Not something that hurt you — that is a different conversation. Something that *bothered* you.

**Hold on to that moment. We will come back to it.**

### My own story

Some time ago I was at a gathering where many people came forward for prayer. I stood back. Then one of the leaders came to me and asked me not to stand around, but to help pray for the people.

Something in me tightened.

The honest truth underneath is this: **I am sometimes afraid to step out and pray for people I don't know.** I doubt myself. I'm not sure I will know what to pray.

I have asked God many times to take that fear away.

**And I know now: this is something God is still shaping in me.**

I believe God can use offence to shape us — if we let Him.

---

## Part one — The invitation

In Matthew 22, Jesus tells a story about a king who prepared a wedding feast for his son. This was not an ordinary party. It was the greatest celebration that kingdom would ever see.

The king sent his servants to call the invited guests. **And they would not come.**

So he sent the servants out again — this time to the street corners.

> **Matthew 22:8-10 — ESV**
> Then he said to his servants, "The wedding feast is ready, but those invited were not worthy. Go therefore to the main roads and invite to the wedding feast as many as you find." And those servants went out into the roads and gathered all whom they found, both bad and good. So the wedding hall was filled with guests.

Notice this: **the people who finally sat at that feast did nothing to get there.** They were on street corners. They were fetched.

The whole chapter is about invitation. And right next to this story, Matthew tells of three times people tried to trap Jesus with a question. Together they show us three ways of saying *no* to God's invitation.

---

## Part two — Three No's

### ① "I'm too busy"

> **Matthew 22:5 — ESV**
> But they paid no attention and went off, one to his farm, another to his business.

Nobody said, *"I don't care about your wedding."* Nobody argued. Nobody was hostile. They just had things to do. A farm to look after. A business that needed them. Good, reasonable excuses.

**It looks just like a busy week. "No thanks — maybe next time."**

**And it is still a No.**

These people did not choose between God and sin. They chose between God and *their farm*. Between God and work that needed doing. And the farm won — not because they decided it was more important, but because they never really decided at all. **Not with a decision, but by drifting along.**

And if someone had stopped them on the road and asked, *"Will I see you at the wedding, or did you turn down the king's invitation?"* — they would have been offended. *"Of course not. I'll come next time. I just have a lot on right now."*

What does that look like for us? A mid-week small group. A prayer meeting. A men's camp or a women's gathering. A church service. A chance to help serve. Each one is an invitation. And each of us has let one slip by with a good excuse.

**This is the first place offence shows up.** Someone touches the thing I have given my week to — and something in me tightens. Because deep down, I know.

### ② "I'll come — but I won't change"

This one is closer to home. Because this man was in the room. He *came*.

> **Matthew 22:11-12 — ESV**
> But when the king came in to look at the guests, he saw there a man who had no wedding garment. And he said to him, "Friend, how did you get in here without a wedding garment?" And he was speechless.

At first this looks unfair. How could someone fetched from a street corner have wedding clothes?

**Here is where we should be careful.** It is often preached that at a royal wedding the host provided the garments, so that nobody had to bring their own. Commentators disagree about this. Some hold that it was the custom; others point out that it cannot be proved, and that the text itself says nothing about it.

The parable does not need it. Notice what the text does say: **the man was speechless.** He had no defence — not poverty, not ignorance, nothing.

**He wanted to be at the feast — but he was not willing to change.**

He thought showing up was the whole point. He trusted what he arrived in. And when the king asked him about it, he had no answer.

Notice how the king speaks to him: **"Friend."**

It sounds warm. But Matthew uses this word only three times, and every time for someone who is in the wrong: the worker who grumbles about his pay (20:13), this man, and Judas in the garden (26:50). It is how you address someone who is getting one last chance to answer — not someone who has done nothing wrong.

And the man does not answer. We will come back to how the parable ends.

### ③ "I already know"

Then the chapter turns. One after another, people come to Jesus with a question — not to learn, but to trap Him.

The attitude underneath is: *I'm not here to be taught. I'm here to test whether you measure up to what I already believe.*

**The question about taxes.** The Pharisees ask whether a Jew should pay taxes to Caesar (Matthew 22:17). If Jesus says yes, He loses the people. If He says no, He is a rebel against Rome.

> **Matthew 22:20-21 — ESV**
> And Jesus said to them, "Whose likeness and inscription is this?" They said, "Caesar's." Then he said to them, "Therefore render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's."

The coin carries Caesar's image — so it belongs to him.

**You carry God's image. So give to God what belongs to God:** your priorities, your awe, and a heart that is willing to learn.

**The question about the resurrection.** The Sadducees did not believe in a resurrection at all. So they told a story about a woman who married seven brothers, one after the other, and asked: in the resurrection, whose wife will she be? (Matthew 22:28)

Jesus does not argue the details. He names the real problem:

> **Matthew 22:29 — ESV**
> You are wrong, because you know neither the Scriptures nor the power of God.

Jesus names two things here:

- **"You know neither the Scriptures."** These were people who could recite the Scriptures and teach them — and still understood God wrongly. *Could I have something wrong, too? Am I willing to learn?*
- **"Nor the power of God."** This is the part we easily read past. *Am I still trying to do this in my own strength?*

**They had a God who was too small.** They tried to shrink Him down to their own limited understanding of who He is.

### What the three No's have in common

Each one was stuck in something:

- The invited guests — in their comfort and their own priorities.
- The man in the hall — in an assumption: that accepting the invitation was the end of the road.
- The Pharisees and the Sadducees — in the way they read the Scriptures. They took offence because of what they believed.

**Not one of them was teachable.**

And when something tightens in me, it usually comes from one of three places:

1. **A shortage of truth** — I understand something wrongly, and I don't know it.
2. **Resistance to change** — I hear what is being asked, and I don't want to.
3. **Trust in my own ability** — I am trying to do this in my own strength, or I don't believe God can change me.

Usually it is more than one of them.

And that is where the parable goes. The king's question is never answered:

> **Matthew 22:13-14 — ESV**
> Then the king said to the attendants, 'Bind him hand and foot and cast him into the outer darkness. In that place there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.' For many are called, but few are chosen.

**This is not a soft ending.** The invitation was lavish — everyone from the street corners was brought in, both bad and good. But it is still an invitation to a wedding. You do not come to a wedding and stay as you were.

That is why we need both **truth and Spirit** to change. Truth shows us where we are wrong. The Spirit gives us the power to change.

Jesus is speaking about worship when He says the Father is looking for people who worship Him *in spirit and truth* (John 4:23-24). But the same two things are at work when He changes us — and you cannot go without either one and expect the other to do the job alone.

## Part three — Where the invitation knocks

Offence usually flares up in one of four areas of our lives.

1. **Your personality** — *"You're criticising who I am."* The thing your husband or wife has mentioned more than once. Or the friend who knows you well enough to say it out loud.
2. **Your identity** — *"You're saying I'm someone other than who I thought I was."* This is not irritation. It feels like the floor is moving under you.
3. **Your theology** — *"You're telling me I have always believed wrongly."* This is exactly where the Pharisees and the Sadducees took offence.
4. **Your values** — *"You're saying what I give my life to is worth less than I think?"* This is the farm. This is the business. This is the first No.

These are not four random sore spots. Look at how Jesus worked with His twelve for three years and you see the same four areas:

- He took Simon, who was often unsteady, and named him Peter — the rock — **identity**.
- A brother who wanted to call down fire on a village became the apostle of love — **personality**.
- *"Who do you say that I am?"* — **theology**.
- He washed their feet while they argued about who was the greatest — **values**.

**Your offence shows you where God is working.** It shows you the area where you still need some shaping. That is where His invitation to change has arrived.

---

## Part four — The garment is already provided

Go back to the man without the wedding garment.

**His problem was not that he had nothing. His problem was that he had nothing to say.**

And what the parable leaves open about the man, the rest of Scripture states plainly: God Himself provides what we need to be made right with Him. We cannot bring our own clothes to His feast. We don't have to.

> **Isaiah 61:10 — ESV**
> I will greatly rejoice in the LORD; my soul shall exult in my God, for he has clothed me with the garments of salvation; he has covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decks himself like a priest with a beautiful headdress, and as a bride adorns herself with her jewels.

And He also says where it is going.

> **Romans 8:29 — ESV**
> For those whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son, in order that he might be the firstborn among many brothers.

**The change has a destination: to look like Jesus.**

And He does not only give us new clothes. He gives us a new heart.

> **Ezekiel 36:26 — ESV**
> And I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you. And I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh.

> **2 Corinthians 5:17 — ESV**
> Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come.

Here is the verse that holds it all together:

> **Philippians 2:12-13 — ESV**
> …work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure.

Look closely. **God gives both the wanting and the doing.** Even the willingness to change comes from Him. We do not produce it ourselves.

But there is still a choice. The man in the hall *could* have put on the garment. He chose not to. Paul says it plainly: *"Put on the Lord Jesus Christ"* (Romans 13:14).

> **He provides everything you need. Your part is to put it on.**

### He wants all of you

The last person to question Jesus in this chapter asks Him which commandment is the greatest (Matthew 22:35-36). It is another trap. But Jesus answers:

> **Matthew 22:37 — ESV**
> You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind.

Heart, and soul, and mind. **He wants all of you.**

And that is why the four areas matter. Every part that God asks for in this verse is a part where offence flares up. Nothing is left out of His invitation.

We don't change and overcome in our own strength. **We are taught and empowered by the Holy Spirit — when we are willing to be teachable.**

## Part five — What a Yes looks like

The Gospels also show us people who said *yes*.

### The woman at Jesus' feet

In Luke 7, Jesus is having a meal at the house of a Pharisee named Simon. A woman known in the town as a sinner comes in. She stands behind Jesus, at His feet, weeping. Her tears fall on His feet. She wipes them with her hair, kisses them, and pours perfume over them.

And Simon takes offence:

> **Luke 7:39 — ESV**
> Now when the Pharisee who had invited him saw this, he said to himself, "If this man were a prophet, he would have known who and what sort of woman this is who is touching him, for she is a sinner."

Simon is the *"I already know"* No, sitting right there at the table. He thought he knew who Jesus was, and who she was.

The woman hid nothing. She knew her need, and she came anyway.

> **Luke 7:47 — ESV**
> Therefore I tell you, her sins, which are many, are forgiven—for she loved much. But he who is forgiven little, loves little.

As a church it is easy to think we are better than others. But Jesus is not looking for people who look righteous. He is looking for people who open their hearts to Him, admit their sin, and are willing to be changed.

**Jesus knows everything about you.** The safe place is not to stay hidden. The safe place is to come behind Jesus and let your tears fall at His feet. Don't let guilt make your heart hard.

We cannot change people. But we can love God, and point people to the heart of Jesus. We want to be a church that serves one another rather than judges one another.

### Mary chose the better part

In Luke 10, Jesus visits the home of Martha and Mary. Martha is busy with all the serving. Mary sits at Jesus' feet and listens.

> **Luke 10:41-42 — ESV**
> "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her."

Mary is the opposite of the first No. **She set her busyness aside to be with Him.**

Both women ended up in the same place: at Jesus' feet. That is what a Yes looks like.

## Part six — The Spirit who changes us

**Offence can be the Holy Spirit pointing out something in you where you need God's power to change.**

That is why we need to be filled with the Holy Spirit. For some, this will be the first time you ask Him to fill you. For others, it is asking Him to fill you again. Being filled with the Spirit is not a once-off event. We can keep asking.

Ask Him for two things in particular:

- **To help you recognise Him quickly** — so that you know: *this is Him. He is here, busy changing me.*
- **For the power to really change.** Willpower alone will not bring the change.

Ask Him to teach you His voice. God often uses other people to help us listen — so pay attention. There is a choice here, and there is responsibility.

> **Deuteronomy 1:6 — ESV**
> The LORD our God said to us in Horeb, "You have stayed long enough at this mountain."

**You have stayed long enough at that mountain.** Whatever that one thing is — the one that came to mind while you were reading.

### The rest of my story

He has not taken away my fear of praying for strangers. Not yet.

But that is exactly where He is still working in me.

**That is not failure. It is an area in me that He is still working on.**

## Your response

Think back to the moment you held on to at the start. Which of the four areas did it touch?

- **Your personality** — how you are
- **Your identity** — who you believe you are, or what others think of you
- **Your theology** — what you are sure about
- **Your values** — what you give your life and time to

You know which one. It came to mind while you were reading.

The prayer is not complicated. Not *"I'll fix it."* Not *"I'll try harder."* Just ask Him:

> **"Lord, what do You want me to change, and how?"**

And then give it to Him:

> **"Lord, I give this area of my life over to You — so that You can change me in it."**

## Questions for reflection

1. Think of a recent moment when something in you tightened. Which of the four areas was it — personality, identity, theology or values?
2. Which of the three No's do you recognise most in yourself: *"I'm too busy"*, *"I'll come but I won't change"*, or *"I already know"*?
3. Is there an invitation from God that you have been letting slip by with a good excuse?
4. Where are you still trying to change in your own strength? What would it look like to "put on" what God has already provided?
5. The woman in Luke 7 hid nothing from Jesus. What would you need to stop hiding in order to come to His feet?
6. Have you asked the Holy Spirit to fill you — for the first time, or again? What stops you?

## Prayer focus

Jesus said it plainly:

> **Luke 17:1 — ESV**
> And he said to his disciples, “Temptations to sin are sure to come, but woe to the one through whom they come!”

**Offence is going to come.** It is not a sign that something has gone wrong — it is part of living alongside other people. Jesus does not say: make it stop. He says two things: it will come, and take care that you are not the one it comes through.

So we do not pray that God would take offence off our path.

We pray that we would recognise it when it comes — that the Holy Spirit would help us see what it opens up in us — and that we would let it do what God intends it to do: change us into the image of His Son.

That we would trust that He has already provided everything we need — and choose to put it on.

And that the Holy Spirit would fill us, teach us His voice, and give us the power to become more like Jesus in every area of our lives.
