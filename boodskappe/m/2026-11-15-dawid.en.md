# The Lies We Leave at the Water's Edge

*Dawid de Greeff · 15 November 2026*

I want to start by asking: **what have you built this year with the material that was put into your hands?**

Some of us will be able to testify to what shifted this year. And some of us have carried stones all year and are standing more or less where we stood a year ago. Not because the material was wrong — because arms get tired, and heads get tired.

I am in that second group this morning. And I want to speak to both groups, because they are not standing as far apart as it looks. Jesus said this to everyone in the room — *Come to Me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest* — Matthew 11. Every one of us is going to need refreshment somewhere on this road.

## The map

I want to put the whole journey on the table. Because you are standing somewhere on it, and what God is asking of you depends on where.

There are four places in this story.

There is **Egypt**. A place God is asking you to leave. It is wearing you out, and it is giving you something you think you cannot do without — and that is what makes it so hard to go. At Egypt God says one thing: **go.**

There is **the sea**. The dead end you hit *after* you obey. The thing you walked away from comes after you, and there is nowhere left to run. At the sea God says: **stand firm, and go forward.**

There is **Marah**, the bitter water. Three days later, when you are through the Sea and you are free, and life still tastes wrong. At Marah God shows us grace: **I will heal this water anyway.**

And there is **Elim**. Twelve springs and seventy palm trees. At Elim God says: **camp here — and then walk on.**

Four places, and four completely different words from the same God. Work out where you are standing. Because if you take Egypt's word at Marah, or Marah's word in Egypt, you will spend a year confused and think God has gone quiet on you.

## One. The dead end He chose for them

Go to the beginning of Exodus 14 — before Pharaoh, before the chariots, before anybody is afraid.

> *Then the LORD said to Moses, "Tell the Israelites to turn back and encamp before Pi Hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea."*
> Exodus 14:1-2

**Turn back. Go and camp there.**

Look at what that place is like. The sea in front. Mountains on both sides. One road in — and that road is the road Pharaoh is going to come down.

It is a dead end. And God sent them into it. He says why in the next verse: *Pharaoh will say of them, they are wandering the land in confusion; the wilderness has shut them in.* He wanted it to look like a mistake.

Hear this — **they did not take a wrong turn.** They were exactly where God told them to be. Obedience walked them straight into a dead end.

If you have ever lain awake thinking: *If God were really in this, I would not be here* — being stuck is not proof that you missed Him.

And He says something else that takes the weight off you: *Pharaoh and all his army will lose against Me;* **the Egyptians will know that I am the LORD** (verse 4).

God orders circumstances to show that He is God — in your life and in the lives of others. **Your dead end may be about more than just you.**

Rahab says it later in Jericho, to two strangers: *we have heard how the LORD dried up the water of the Red Sea for you.* She was never there, but it turned her towards God.

**What God does at your water may have a ripple effect that reaches others.**

### It got worse before it got better

Something we do not say often enough in church.

**Obeying God made Israel's circumstances worse before they got better.**

They were safer the week before. Nobody was chasing them and they had food. The week they obeyed is the week **an army came after them.**

So if you take the step He is asking of you — if you end the thing, or make the call, or walk away from the arrangement that has been holding you up — the first things that happen may be that it gets harder. Money gets tight. The house goes quiet.

Harder is not a sign that you got it wrong. **That is Exodus chapter fourteen.**

And some of us are not at the sea yet. You are still in Egypt, and you know it. God is not saying stand firm to you. Your challenge may be the biggest one on this map. **God is asking you to come out of Egypt.**

### What came out of them

Look at what happens the moment the dust appears on the horizon.

> *It would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the desert!*
> Exodus 14:12

Hear who is saying it. These are not unbelievers. The blood is on their doorframes, they watched ten plagues, and they walked out of Egypt as free people three days ago.

And now they stand there and say out loud that **slavery looked safer.**

That is what pressure does. Pressure makes a person look differently at his **"past".**

**That sentence — "slavery looked safer" — is a lie.** Egypt was not better. There were whips, and there were baby boys in the river. But standing at that water with the chariots coming, the lie did not sound like a lie.

It sounded true.

And that is how it always sounds. Unbelief almost never sounds like unbelief. It sounds level-headed. It sounds like this:

> ***It was not as bad as everyone makes out.***
>
> ***It works for other people. It has never worked for me.***
>
> ***If I were really free, I would not still want it.***
>
> ***If I had handled it better, it would not be like this at home.***
>
> ***This arrangement is how I survive. I cannot afford to let it go.***
>
> ***I will deal with it after the holidays.***

Those sentences feel like honesty. They feel like being realistic. And every one of them is unbelief dressed up as the truth. Someone has carried one of them around for years and called it realism.

### Stand firm

And then Moses says the first true thing anyone has said all day: **Stand right where you are. Wait and see. The LORD will fight for you.**

Some of us, when we hear *stand firm*, **hear**: do nothing. That is not what it says.

The Hebrew word means **take your position.** Plant your feet. That is what you say to a soldier, not to a spectator.

And I know that is right, because two verses later God says: *tell the people to move on* — and then tells Moses to lift his staff. Same conversation, minutes apart.

So here is what standing firm means: **stop doing the thing that is not working.**

Israel could not beat Egypt. No chariots, no swords, no army, no plan. And God never once asked them to fight. That morning the battle was not theirs.

David said it with a stone in his hand: ***the battle is the LORD's*** — **and then he ran at the giant.**

Hear both halves. **The fight is His. The step is yours.** God does what we cannot do. He does not do what He told us to do.

Hendrik puts it in five words: *"Jesus replaces fears with faith."* That is the Red Sea.

### And go forward

I will not ask anyone one thing that we as leaders are not willing to do ourselves. I had to go and stand in front of that sea myself, examine myself, and cry out to God. When I say stand firm — I am saying it to myself first.

Those of us standing in front of the sea must take our positions and get ready to move forward. And do not wait for the new year.

**Breakthrough comes through the next act of obedience — the one the Holy Spirit has already prompted you about.**

Not a new one, and not a bigger one. **The one that is already in your heart** — the thing that stirs in your quiet time, that you keep pushing aside.

God is not standing with His arms folded, waiting for you to perform before He moves. **He has already opened the road** and He is standing at the edge of it. The delay is not His reluctance. He will not drag anyone through a sea against their will.

Victory comes through a hundred small obediences. It almost never comes through one big moment.

And look at how the chapter ends.

> *And when the Israelites saw the mighty hand of the LORD displayed against the Egyptians, the people feared the LORD and **put their trust in Him** and in Moses His servant.*
> Exodus 14:31

That was God's purpose with Israel at the sea. Not the crossing, but **faith.** The crossing was only how He worked it in.

**In short:** Being stuck is not proof that you missed God. Obedience can make things harder first. Standing firm means stopping what is not working, and taking the next step.

## Two. Bitter water

Israel walks through on dry ground. On the other side they turn round and see their enemies lying dead on the sand. Miriam takes a tambourine, the women dance, and the whole nation sings the first song in the Bible.

At the sea they sang because they had seen what God had done. Jehoshaphat's singers, in 2 Chronicles 20, walked out ahead of the army before anything had happened — they sang about who He ís.

**When everything in your life is upside down and crooked, sing of His power, His promises and His faithfulness — of who He is.**

Hendrik puts it like this: *"What then is faith? It is a firm assurance that the things we look forward to will indeed happen."*

And then, the next verse.

> *Then Moses led Israel from the Red Sea and they went into the Desert of Shur. For three days they travelled in the desert without finding water.*
> Exodus 15:22

You would expect the next chapter to tell of the Promised Land.

**But** three days after the greatest thing God had ever done in front of their eyes, there is nothing to drink. The tambourines are barely back in the bags. And it exposes a lie that lives in good Christian people.

> ***If I follow Him properly, my journey will go smoothly.***

If we are honest, most of us believe that to a greater or lesser degree. **A dry season after a moment of breakthrough is not God leaving you, or losing interest. It is the next part of the road, and it is on the map.**

### The water they could not drink

What can be worse than three days without water, and being really **thirsty**?

On the third day they come over a rise and **there is water.** You can see it — the children start running, everyone pushing to get to the front. They scoop it up, they put it in their mouths, and they spit it out.

It is undrinkable. The place is called Marah, and Marah means **bitter.**

To **hope** and then be **disappointed** is far worse than the drought. It makes a person bitter.

And look at what they do with it. In verse 24 they turn on Moses — not on God, on their leader, who is closest to them.

**Is it not true that bitterness turns you against those closest to you, and hits them hardest of all?** It is your husband. It is your boss. It is the church. It is your mother. It is the person who did not call.

And right there, with that taste in their mouths, there is a word from Hendrik that fits: *"Don't look at the pain, look into God's eyes."*

### When bitter becomes your name

There is a lie at Marah and it is the most dangerous one. **This taste is permanent, and it is someone else's fault.**

Here is where that lie ends.

Years later a woman comes back from Moab and arrives in Bethlehem — the town she left ten years before. Her husband is dead. Both her sons are dead. The town comes out to meet her and calls her by her name, Naomi — and she stops them.

> *"Don't call me Naomi," she told them. "Call me **Mara** — bitter — because the Almighty has made my life very bitter."*
> Ruth 1:20

Hear the difference now, because everything turns on it. Bitter things happen to every one of us. Naomi took it one step further.

**What went wrong is that she took the bitter water and made it her name.**

Ask yourself what story you tell within ten minutes of meeting someone new.

And hear how her story ends, because it is for those who are further down that road than they wanted to be. At the end of the book, when the baby is born, the women of the town say: ***a son has been born to Naomi.***

She never changed her name back herself. **The community changed her name back for her.**

That is what a congregation is for. When you cannot remember who you are, we remind you: that you have forgiveness, that you belong to Him, that you were not always like this, and that He has a future for you. And we keep saying it until you believe it about yourself again.

### He healed the water in front of them

So what does God do at Marah?

He shows Moses a piece of wood. Moses throws it into the water. And the water becomes sweet.

Notice — God did not make Israel walk five kilometres further to a better spring. **He healed the water that was already in front of them.**

**Most of us have the wrong idea of what God's refreshment looks like.** We think it means the pressure lifts, the circumstances change, the problem disappears. Here the desert did not change and the journey did not get shorter. **The water changed, and they could drink it.**

**Refreshment is not the pressure lifting. It is God putting back into you what the road took out of you, so that you can keep going, all the way to the end.**

Paul asked three times for his thorn to be taken away. It was not taken away. What he got instead was: *My grace is sufficient for you, for My power is made perfect in weakness* (2 Corinthians 12:9).

That is refreshment. Not the thorn gone — strength at a time when you have no strength left.

### He healed it anyway

They complained. They turned on their leader. They did not pray, they did not repent, they did not even fix their attitude.

**And God healed the water anyway.**

He did not stand there with folded arms waiting for an apology. **That is grace in action** — not a reward for good behaviour, but a gift to people who did not deserve it and did not even ask nicely.

He does it again in the next chapter — they complain about food, and He sends bread from heaven. And again the chapter after that — they complain about water, and He splits a rock open. Three times in three chapters. Every time they complain first. Every time He provides.

This is not a slip. **This is His pattern.**

It does not mean complaining works with God — it means the opposite. **His giving was never tied to their emotion in the first place.** He gave out of who He is.

### And then the key

And then, only then, He tells them His Name — and gives them something to live by.

> *If you listen carefully to the voice of the LORD your God and do what is right in His eyes… **for I am the LORD who heals you.**"*
> Exodus 15:26

Look at the order, because it corrects something many of us have the wrong way round.

**First He heals the water. Without conditions.** Then He says: **this is how you are to live**, so that you are not standing at bitter water every second month.

**Grace is the door. Obedience is the road.** He does not withhold your healing until you have earned it, and He is not going to leave you standing at bitter water for the rest of your life.

And notice where this Name is given. Not at the springs, and not on the far side of the Red Sea with the tambourines playing. **The first time in Scripture that God calls Himself your Healer, He is standing at bitter water, surrounded by people who are complaining.**

> ***I am the LORD who heals you.***

### What turns you towards Him

There is one line from the Sermon on the Mount that ties all of this together.

> *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled.*
> Matthew 5:6

Hungry, and then filled.

Notice that they are not hungry for an easier week, and not for a feeling. **For righteousness** — to be right and clean before Him. That is the hunger that turns a person towards God.

It does not say *blessed are those who have got it right.* It says hungry. And hunger is not an achievement. **It is the one thing a person who keeps falling can still have.** The water at Marah was healed, after all, while they were still complaining about it.

**God is withholding nothing from you.** The water has already been poured out, and there is nothing standing there waiting for you to earn it.

But a person who has been drinking elsewhere all week arrives at that water without thirst. Such a person is not shut out. Just not thirsty. **A divided heart cannot drink what God has already poured out** — it is not God refusing him. It is a man who is full of something else.

God is not asking you to be perfect before He will refresh you. **He is asking whether you are still hungry for Him.**

And some of us may realise that **the hunger itself is gone** — that you have long since stopped longing for God the way you once longed for Him. If that is true, then that is your sentence. Say it to Him in those words: *Lord, I have stopped longing for You.*

**In short:** Refreshment is not the circumstances changing — it is God giving back what the road took out of you. All that is asked of you is hunger.

## The call

Most of us carry a lie-sentence around with us. We are not talking about sin — about a sentence, one of those above or your own version of it, that you say to yourself so often it has started to sound like the truth.

Bring that sentence and lay it down before God. You do not have to make it sound good, or explain where it came from, or have a plan for it.

**You can be hungry for God and still be holding on to the thing you lean on.** Most of us are. He is not asking you to let go of it immediately. **He is asking you to be honest with Him about it.**

And you may not know yet what yours is. That is all right, because finding your sentence is not your work either. **Ask the Holy Spirit to show you the sentence you have been calling the truth.** He is the One who reveals what is hidden.

Be quiet for a while and ask Him: *Lord, what am I telling myself that is not from You?* And when He shows it to you, throw it into the sea.

That is what the Bible calls **repentance.** And it opens God's refreshment to you.

> *Repent, then, and turn to God, so that your sins may be wiped out, that times of refreshing may come from **the presence of the Lord**, and that He may send the Messiah, who has been appointed for you — even Jesus.*
> Acts 3:19-20

Peter said this in Jerusalem, to people who had been standing in the crowd that handed Jesus over.

Revival. Refreshing. Renewal. It is one Greek word that every translation renders differently — and look where it comes from. Refreshment does not come through your circumstances changing. **It comes through the presence of the Lord in your life.**

That is why you already have access to it, even though nothing in your life has changed yet.

## Elim

After the bitter water, this is what happens to Israel.

> *Then they came to Elim, where there were twelve springs and seventy palm trees, and they camped there near the water.*
> Exodus 15:27

Twelve springs — one for each tribe. Nobody has to fight for a place at the water. Seventy palm trees: shade, in a desert.

God does not send them straight into the next battle. He says: camp here.

The next chapter begins like this: *the Israelites set out from Elim.* **Elim was water on the road, not the end of the road.**

So do not wait until you are at Elim before you breathe. **He has water for you at the bitter place too.**

Jesus promises something even better than Elim: *the water I give them will become in them a spring of water welling up to eternal life* (John 4:14).

He does not take you to the spring. **He puts the spring inside you.**

## When you do not feel refreshed

Keep these four things close, because in four weeks you are going to need them.

**His Word** — what He said is true, even when you are afraid.

**Your step** — if you obeyed, something real happened. Obedience is visible. Feeling is not.

**His Name** — *"I am the Lord who heals you"* says something about Him, not about your week.

**Each other** — the body remembers for you when you cannot remember yourself.

>> Refreshment is not a place. It is a Person. And that Person is Jesus.
