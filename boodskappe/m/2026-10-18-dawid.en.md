# Built In Together by What We Behold

*Dawid & Annemarie de Greeff · 18 October 2026*

Last week we started with a dull little stone lying on the bank of the Orange River near Hopetown. In 1867 a fifteen-year-old boy picked it up because it looked interesting. He did not know what it was. That stone had no light of its own — it had to be found, carried away and cut before it could shine. Today we call it the Eureka diamond, and about half of it was cut away to get there.

That stone is us. God came looking for us first. He loved us first, He called us, He walked that riverbed and saw us lying there and picked us up. And He wants us to turn to Him and hand ourselves over so that He can cut us.

The other thing we saw last week is simple: **we become what we behold.** If I sit on my phone all day, I slowly start to look like my phone. If we keep turning towards Jesus and keep looking at who He is and what the Word says about Him, we slowly start to look like Him.

So today we finish the picture, with two questions. If He has picked us up and we are in His hand and we are looking at Jesus — can we still miss Him? And what does God build out of people who look at Him together?

## One. We can look at Him and still miss Him

> *"And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another. For this comes from the Lord who is the Spirit."*
> 2 Corinthians 3:18

Some translations say *reflecting*, and some say *beholding*. Both are in the word. But the one we are after today is the second one: while we behold Him, we are changed.

We are always looking at something. If we are not looking at Jesus, we are looking at the world, or we are looking at ourselves. Ecclesiastes 1:8 says the eye is never satisfied with seeing and the ear is never filled with hearing. We never get tired of that. We always want more of it.

What may surprise us is that church people — people like us, sitting in this room — can look in Jesus' direction and still miss Him. There are three ways to do it.

### The wish list

This is when our faith shifts from who He is to what He can do for us. We come with our needs, our fears and our plans so that He can fix our circumstances. We are not really looking at God; we are looking at our list. And when He says no, or says wait, or the thing we prayed for does not happen, our faith dries up — because we decided in advance what He was for.

### The mirror

Last week we said we see Him as in a mirror. But you can look into that same mirror and see yourself instead of Him. "How well am I doing? How many verses do I know? How often am I in the Word? How good am I to people?" That sounds like a healthy question. Scripture calls it pride. And that same mirror has another side that sounds nothing like pride: "I am a failure." That is self-condemnation, and it can even look like humility. Both of them are me. Neither of them is Him.

### The textbook

Many of us love hours in the Word and know a great deal about Jesus. He said this to the people who knew the Scriptures best in His day:

> *"You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness about me, yet you refuse to come to me that you may have life."*
> John 5:39-40

Study and searching are not the problem. Stopping there is.

A wish list, a mirror and a textbook all end up in the same place: a Jesus shaped to our own taste. A Jesus who looks and acts the way we would like Him to. What we are asked to behold is who He actually is — His love, His holiness, His grace and His truth.

**In short:** We can look in Jesus' direction and still miss Him, because a wish list, a mirror and a textbook all point back at ourselves.

## Two. His face before His hand

*Annemarie shares*

True beholding seeks His face before it seeks His hand. It is not only about us knowing Jesus — the demons know Him too. They know the whole Word. So what makes the difference? We can know the whole Word and still only know *about* Jesus. We are after knowing Him.

Seeking His face first does not mean we never ask Him for anything. It means there is an order. When the disciples asked how they should pray, Jesus did not begin with the bread. He began with the Name, the kingdom and the will, and only then with "give us today our daily bread." There is order in everything God does — He did not make a fish before there was water for it to swim in.

> *"One thing have I asked of the Lord, that will I seek after: that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, to gaze upon the beauty of the Lord… You have said, 'Seek my face.' My heart says to you, 'Your face, Lord, do I seek.'"*
> Psalm 27:4, 8

Most of us know the moment, even if we have never thought of it that way. You see a sunset, or a bunch of flowers, or the clouds, and there is a second where you simply stand still and say, *look how beautiful that is.* In that second there is no wish list and no to-do list. You are not asking the sunset for anything. You are just looking.

That is part of beholding. And notice what the low sun does: it turns everything that golden colour. If you stand and look at it long enough, you will see it on your face too.

Why does it matter? Because when I start at His face, my will begins to change into His will. All of us are here for a short while. We were not here when it started and there will be a time when we are not here any more, and the world will carry on. He is the only One who has always been here, and His plan is the only plan that lasts to the very end. All of ours will pass. So His is the only plan worth being part of — and standing and looking at His face is how my will moves over to His will, and stays there.

Compare that with the prayer that only reaches for His hand. "Lord, here is my fear. Here is my need. Here is my plan. You are the only one who can do this, so please do it." Underneath, the aim of that prayer is to get God to do my will — because I have already decided what should change. The result is a short relief and then a long worry, because nothing moves while I am waiting for Him to move it.

We behold Jesus because He is worth beholding. We behold plenty of other things, and none of them are like our God. Do this, follow this plan, get this job, be this thin, wear these clothes — all of it promises to be enough and none of it is. Look at His face, not at your list.

**In short:** There is an order. Start with who He is and your will moves to His; start with your list and you are left holding the list.

## Three. He builds us in together

Did you notice the two words in that verse? *We all.* Beholding is not a me-thing. It is an us-thing.

A lot of people say, "Jesus and I are fine. I don't need the church." But Paul says Christ loved the church and gave Himself up for her (Ephesians 5:25). Put the two together. If beholding Him makes me more like Him, and He loved the church enough to die for her, how can I say I do not want to be built into God's church and to love His people? Then I cannot stay a loose stone.

> *"As you come to him, a living stone rejected by men but in the sight of God chosen and precious, you yourselves like living stones are being built up as a spiritual house."*
> 1 Peter 2:4-5

Peter keeps going. The stone the builders rejected became the cornerstone; and to those who will not obey the word, that same stone is something to trip over. One stone, two outcomes. The same Jesus is either the cornerstone you build your life on, or the stone you fall over. We heard the same thing in the Offence series: the gospel itself can be something we stumble on, because it asks us to do what we do not want to do.

A cornerstone is the stone everything else is lined up against. So when we look at Jesus, we are lining ourselves up with God's plan and God's will rather than our own. And then Peter says why the whole thing exists:

> *"But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession, that you may proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvellous light."*
> 1 Peter 2:9

*Proclaim His excellencies.* That is why the wall exists. Not for us. So that the world sees His mercy, His grace, His love, His justice, His holiness.

And here is how He does the cutting. Proverbs 27:17 says iron sharpens iron, and so one person sharpens another. God uses the people sitting next to you to take off the faces that still have to shine. Every time we choose forgiveness over taking offence, another facet is cut. Every time we choose humility over being right, another one. Every time we choose His will over ours, another one. Each rough edge that comes off is more of His light reflected.

There is a wall at the front of this building. Jurie once looked at it and said some of those bricks are fairly crooked and the lines are not straight. He is right. And that is the whole point. If I measure myself against the brick next to me, all I see is how crooked he is. If I measure myself against the cornerstone, I see how imperfect I am — and how much grace God has for both of us.

That is why Jesus asks why we see the speck in our brother's eye and not the log in our own (Matthew 7:3). So we do not look at the person next to us. We look at Jesus together.

>> Unity is not that we agree with each other. Unity is that we agree on who we are looking at.

**In short:** He builds people who look at Him into one wall, and He uses the people next to you to cut the rough edges away.

## Four. He looks deeper — and He does not throw the stone away

*Annemarie shares*

God sent Samuel to anoint the next king, and Samuel looked at Jesse's big, obvious sons and was sure every time. He was wrong every time.

> *"Do not look on his appearance or on the height of his stature… For the Lord sees not as man sees: man looks on the outward appearance, but the Lord looks on the heart."*
> 1 Samuel 16:7

The one who was out in the field with the sheep was the one. And that is the man who later prayed Psalm 139 — search me, know my heart, see if there is any grievous way in me.

Here is something I did not know about diamonds until we went looking for one. Diamonds have flaws on the inside. You cannot see them. The man who cuts them uses a heavy magnifying glass and studies the inside of the stone, and then he can see the little marks in there. On the outside we can all shine. There is a layer only He can see.

And the beautiful thing is what He does with what He finds. He does not say, *sorry, you are too flawed, if I cut all of that away there will be nothing left.* He does not throw the stone away. Somehow He cuts in a way that leaves something beautiful behind, every time.

Here it is worth following Paul's life. Think of the shipwrecks and the snake bite and everything else — he sounds like a man made of iron. But put his letters in the order he wrote them rather than the order they sit in the Bible, and watch what happens to how he describes himself.

Around AD 49 he writes to the Galatians: *Paul, an apostle.* About six years later, to the Corinthians: *the least of the apostles* (1 Corinthians 15:9). Around AD 60, to the Ephesians: *the very least of all the saints* (Ephesians 3:8). And near the end:

> *"Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the foremost. But I received mercy for this reason, that in me, as the foremost, Jesus Christ might display his perfect patience as an example to those who were to believe in him."*
> 1 Timothy 1:15-16

*I am.* Not *I was.* He is older, he has travelled further, he has seen more and he has been looking at Jesus longer — and he is going down, not up. The closer he gets, the more clearly he sees his own flaws, and the bigger the grace looks.

>> This is the question to carry into the week: is it going up, or is it going down?

And when the stone next to you looks crooked, look at the cornerstone. There is another way to ask the same thing: *how does Jesus see that person?* That is a big challenge, and the only way to do it is to ask Him for help, because this flesh wants its own way, its own people and its own will.

One practical thing. Sometimes you sit down to seek His face and you are blank — there is so much going on that you cannot think of a thing to say. So mark the praise in your Bible. Hannah prayed, "There is none holy like the Lord, there is none besides you, there is no rock like our God" (1 Samuel 2:2). David blessed the Lord in front of the whole assembly: "Yours, Lord, is the greatness and the power and the glory" (1 Chronicles 29:11). The Word is full of people who did not come with a list. Open it and ask Him to show you a prayer, and start there — even if you ask Him for nothing at all.

**In short:** He sees the flaws nobody else sees, and He does not throw the stone away — and the closer you get to Him, the smaller you become in your own eyes.

## Come and behold Him together

We can look at Jesus and still miss Him: a wish list, a mirror, a textbook. So we start at His face and not at our list. And when the stone next to us looks crooked, we look at the cornerstone first.

God builds people who look at Jesus into one wall, lined up on Him, in unity with each other. Not so that we can admire the wall. Peter says it is so that we can show everyone how good He is, and Paul says in Ephesians 2:22 that we are being built together into a place where God lives by His Spirit. The wall exists so that God is at home in it and is seen in it.

And He puts the lonely into a family. Psalm 68:6 says God settles the solitary in a home. Hebrews 10:24-25 says to keep paying attention to one another — how *we* spur *each other* on to love and good works — and not to give up meeting together. Jesus said everyone will know we are His disciples by the way we love one another (John 13:35). Not by how I love God on my own. By how we forgive each other, stay humble with each other, and refuse to take offence.

> *"They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads. And night will be no more. They will need no light of lamp or sun, for the Lord God will be their light, and they will reign for ever and ever."*
> Revelation 22:4-5

One day we will stand in front of Him, face to face. For some of us it will happen when we lie our heads down; some of us will still be here the day He comes on the clouds.

He will know you because He bought you. That is settled, and it does not depend on how well you looked at Him.

But what He sees on you is what you have been looking at. Stand in that light and let it change you, and His image and His name burn into you. Watch the world all day and something else does. So the question is not whether He will know you. The question is: when you come before Him, will you be shining with Him, or with something else?

>> Come and behold Him together. We become what we behold.
