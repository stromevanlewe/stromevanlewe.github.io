# First the Heart

*Hendrik van der Merwe · 1 November 2026*

We are starting with purity this morning.

But I do not want to start with purity before we know why we end up there. Because often in church we sit and we want to do something quickly, because we want to go and do something quickly. We do not sit still and learn at the feet of the Lord *how* to get there.

It is not: right, I do this thing and this thing and this thing. **You get the heart of something first.**

And that is how Jesus always walked among people. He made them feel important. He did not make them feel they had to do something first in order to be with Him. He taught them to receive the heart, and *then* to go and do something.

This matters, because if you catch the heart, you walk a long way on it. And if you lose something, your heart can be filled again — because you took the time to get the heart of the thing first.

## The boy and the yearbook

We watched a film last night. It was not a terribly good film, but do you know what was amazing?

This boy wanted so badly to stand out at school. He wanted to be in the yearbook — in America you have a yearbook. He wanted to be one of the most important people.

And he had this weird friend. This friend stood by him through everything.

Then he lost everything. And the everything he lost turned out to be the friend.

Until he went and put it right and said: "Listen, this stuff is not that important. Actually, *you* were the treasure. And I want to mend the friendship."

And then they are in the yearbook together, as best friends.

It is really beautiful. And that is how I want to look at purity this morning — **through the lens of family.** We do not want to look at each other and see what faults we have. We want to strengthen each other to come through things, to be like Jesus.

Because that is what matters most. If we are in church and we do not become like Jesus, then we miss everything. We can become like *each other*. We can imitate one another. **But we will not be like Christ if we do not imitate Him first.**

## Our own standards

Here is what people do.

You arrive at a comfortable place where you feel comfortable, where you have what you want and do what you want. And then you hand those standards to other people as the mark they must reach.

That is actually selfish, isn't it?

In ourselves, in our flesh, much of what we do is really a position of comfort: **I will accept you if you do it the way I do it.** It is sometimes like circles of friends too — if you do as I do, then I can associate with you and be part of you.

And you do not even know it, because it is part of your armour. It is part of the walls you build — not the church — to protect yourself, and your own identity, and your own will.

**But Jesus is different.**

## One. You are born into the kingdom

Look at how Jesus does it, at how He drew people.

> *"And He said: Truly I tell you, unless you change and become like little children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven."*
> Matthew 18:3

Here is the first condition. And this condition is so amazing.

Look at the language Jesus speaks. When Jesus came to earth He used the word *kingdom* constantly. Go and look in the gospels — the kingdom is like, the kingdom is like, the kingdom is like. He was teaching people what the kingdom is, because they did not know.

But He was also the revelation of it. What happened when they brought the woman caught in adultery, when they wanted to throw the stones? Jesus wrote something. **He is the kingdom.**

And this verse says: *unless you change.* That change comes from God. It is by His Holy Spirit, where people cannot see it, that He works a change of heart. That is what He means when He says born again.

**You cannot receive this from any person.** The church does not give it to you either. Jesus gives it to you. He is the Creator, He is the One who plants it, He is the One who gives it.

### Like a child

*Unless you become like little children.*

It carries such beautiful symbolic weight. You must become like a child. You must be born. Your heart must change in order to understand this church. You must know who He is, and what God's heart for you is.

Just as parents receive a baby and learn from this child — and the nappies are not a problem, and the growing up is not a problem. Because something has been born out of them, husband and wife, and they stand up for this child. There is a bond, and there is life, and there is a lifelong commitment.

And if you are born into the church, it begins with Christ. He really is everything.

It is important to know that it begins with *Him*. And it is important to be able to say: *Lord, I do not know You in this matter.* When we know the kingdom and we come up against difficulty, we go to Jesus first and ask: Lord, how am I to handle this thing?

And I have the right to ask, because I am born of the Father. I know His voice. I know His way.

**That makes me a child. That is the first step.**

## Two. A child belongs in a family

Jesus taught people about the kingdom. He did signs and wonders. He was the revelation of everything.

But in His own time He did not plant a church.

He had disciples. He showed us discipleship. He had disciples who, when the Holy Spirit came, would become apostles — and there are prophets, and the gifts were laid down. A body was laid down to invest in this kingdom.

So if you are born, then you must be in a family.

Why? Why can I not simply say: I do my own thing, I have the kingdom, I am in the family?

Because Jesus Himself is the example. He shared with Peter and said that on the day of Pentecost something would come down from the Holy Spirit to make them church. He added the Holy Spirit to it, so that we would know *how* Jesus builds.

We know the practical side of doing church. We learn it in the Bible, we know how to appoint leaders. **But there is a heart that flows out of someone who has been born into Jesus** — a life that understands what He wants.

And there are many churches with people sitting in them who are not connected.

This is the wonder we so often miss. We do not realise what a miracle God has given us in being part of the body — to change, to become pure, to receive a different character.

**That happens in the church.** And it ought to happen in the church. It is like a family sorting out problems, like a family giving counsel and wisdom. And it brings us to a place where we *want* to change, in love.

So we receive the kingdom like a child, but we grow up in a church into maturity — where I can carry the responsibility of being grown up and of changing.

> *"But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things will be added to you."*
> Matthew 6:33

Just before that verse Jesus says: do not be like the pagans. They seek after clothes and after food — those are their first concerns.

Now we in the church have those concerns too, believe it or not. None of us arrived here this morning without clothes. It *is* a need.

But our hearts changed to **seek His heart first**, and then everything else falls into place as it should.

> *"If you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved."*
> Romans 10:9

That is how simple it is. But the Lord knows the sincerity of our hearts.

## Three. From childhood to maturity

Purity is something I receive from the Lord. **But it is also something to grow into.**

Listen to this congregation.

> *"Dear brothers and sisters, we want you to know about the undeserved goodness of God that the churches in Macedonia received. Although they were severely tested by hardship, they remained overflowing with joy, and their deep poverty overflowed into rich generosity. I testify that they gave according to their ability, and even beyond their ability, entirely on their own. They urgently pleaded with us for the privilege of sharing in this ministry to the believers in Judea. And it was not as we expected. First they gave themselves to the Lord, and then to us, so that they might act according to the will of God."*
> 2 Corinthians 8:1-5

Do you see it? These people were poor.

And yet they said to Jerusalem — which was really the centre where everything was happening — we cannot sit still. How can the Lord use us? What do we have? What can we put together, and how can God bless it to bless this church?

**That is a mature stage.** These are people who do not look at their abilities, and do not look at their shortcomings, but who know God's word, and understand how God has walked a road with them, and understand what His promises to them are.

In childhood it is lovely to know what God has for me and what He can do for me. **But it takes a grown-up to walk out what God has planned for you.**

There must be children in a family. There must be people who are growing — everyone will have their time. But there must also be grown-ups who walk it out in this life, which is sometimes hard, until God becomes real to us.

And *that* is the church. The church is not only receiving like little children. It is being mature and following God, no matter what happens. If you have too little — God is enough. If you do not know how to do something — the Lord will train you.

### And then Paul uses them as the example

> *"So we urged Titus, just as he had earlier made a beginning, to bring also to completion this act of grace on your part. But since you excel in everything — in faith, in speech, in knowledge, in complete earnestness and in the love we have kindled in you — see that you also excel in this grace of giving. I am not commanding you, but I want to test the sincerity of your love by comparing it with the earnestness of others."*
> 2 Corinthians 8:6-8

In the one congregation there were people who had nothing, and something of God rose up out of them to become an example that could be carried over to the other congregations.

Here Paul speaks as a grown-up: it is like children who have gifts, but this thing must function *together*. It must come to a place where what we do comes out of one heart, one mind and one love.

And notice what he does *not* say. *I am not commanding you.*

**The kingdom is a standard.** The heart and love of Jesus is a standard. If Jesus says to you: love that man — and he is not yet with the Lord — then the Lord will use you in such a way that he understands through you that Jesus wants his heart and his life.

The kingdom does not work the way people say it must. **It works the way God says it must.**

## Four. Imitating

> *"Imitate me, just as I also imitate Christ."*
> 1 Corinthians 11:1

Here we are given a sequence.

I have used this example before, and it stays interesting to me. If I teach a parrot to say *I am a policeman* — once he has finished saying the words, is he a policeman?

He is a feather duster.

**So if we sing and we talk but we do not have the heart, we never arrive at surrender.**

It takes maturity, growing up out of childlike faith, to imitate and to grow. And then at some point you come to where you *want* to be mature.

It is just like a relationship. I can remember coming to a place in my heart where I wanted to marry Engela. Those of you who are older, you know. There is that song that says *I don't wanna play house.* It is lovely to be a child in the house, it is lovely to be free and have no responsibility.

And here one must be clear: **being a child does not mean I can do anything I like.** Being a child means you are free to imitate the maturity the Lord has put in your path.

Alongside the Word, and alongside the Holy Spirit, God sets a family — so that you have someone to imitate, and so that your heart can come to the place where you can be built in as God wants you.

> *"So that we may all reach unity in the faith and in the knowledge of the Son of God, and become mature, attaining to the whole measure of the fullness of Christ."*
> Ephesians 4:13

Who is going to do this, by faith? **All of us.**

This verse shows us that we as a church — we who imitate, we who have children and also grown-ups and leaders — are moving towards a place of purity.

And I almost want to say we must know the fight. We must understand what is happening to us. We must understand that the Lord does not simply want me to change because I am bad.

**I must change because I want to be like Jesus. And why do I want to be like Jesus? Because He is purity for me.**

Because I am forgiven, and because Jesus forgave everything at the cross, my old works no longer determine who I am. Jesus determines it.

## Purity is knowing who He is

So purity is not: I was bad, now I am getting good.

**Purity is: I know who Jesus is. I know how He works.**

I remember asking Engela to marry me. I told her to close her eyes, there in Kimberley, and then I drove the most terrible loops. I measured how many minutes it took. I wound her heart up like that.

It was not the smartest occasion. To me it was very beautiful and very loving. But I thought: I am not the man with the biggest dollars, **but I'm gonna make her remember this.**

Romeo sang under the window for Juliet.

I serenaded her — that was my way of serenading, because I have a golden voice. The sooner I stop, the better.

And when we sat down there, I had taken oak leaves and decorated, and little flowers, whatever I had. But I also put the Word of God there, at Matthew 19: *what God has joined together, let no one separate.* I put the rings on the Bible and asked her: will you?

And I can testify to you how God has held us together through many things.

**God has His own way of waking up your heart to follow Him.** It is not the same for everybody. But, boy, are you gonna love Him when He does it.

To wake your heart up, to become His child, it is going to take closing your eyes and letting Him lead. How many times have you asked: where are we going? And you do not know.

## There is nobody like you

Do you know how good it is to yield?

The devil works so that you do not yield to God — that is the bad thing. Sin and the power of sin make it so that you do not have a heart to surrender to the Lord.

But there is a shifting that the Lord wants to do. He is gracious to us, He gives us His Spirit, and then He comes and He wants to change us — so that we can be mature, and understand how God's kingdom works, and understand to whom I must bind and give myself.

And when I then stand up, I stand up as someone who says: I thought like a child, I reasoned like a child, but now that I have built myself in there is the pulse of the Holy Spirit — of people who watch over me, people who teach me, people who lead me.

There is a song that says *I want to scream and shout.*

People, you do not know where I was. You do not know how bound my heart was and how I believed lies. And the Lord gives me the strength to say: I want to imitate, I want to follow Christ, and that is what makes me free.

Not my gift. Not how special I am. Not my past.

God wants you to become a light. Because did you know — **you are the only one.** Every one of us sitting here: you are the only one who can radiate God's kingdom and God's salvation and God's love the way *you* are meant to.

There is nobody like you.

There are specific people who will come to repentance if you are mature in the Lord — people others may never even reach. You have a testimony that will wake people's hearts through Christ in you, which others cannot do.

## The invitation

I want to give an invitation.

Go and sit with someone. Just put your hand on their shoulder. While you pray, ask the Lord: let me go and sit with someone. Pray for connection, and love, and trust. Ask that the Lord would touch this person.

Be a little bold. You do not have to pray anything. Just sit there.

And hear me well: this is not *I think you are weak.* This morning it is: **I want to be pure like you, Jesus. I don't have all the answers.**

It is what we try to do with our children when they have done wrong. We try to help them understand: this is not who you are, this is not how you should act. And if they walk away, they should be built up rather than feeling bad.

That is the idea.

We are in a town where everyone comes to those moments where they have to surrender. And it comes often — not only the one time when you say: here I am, Lord, I am now a child.

Do you know how many times a grown-up has to admit that he is wrong? And that he is perhaps not carrying the right heart about something?

We want to sit in a congregation where people feel they can surrender.

>> Purity is not getting better. Purity is knowing who Jesus is.
