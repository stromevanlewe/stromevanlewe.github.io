# Offence Is a Heart Condition

*A message by Hendrik van der Merwe — Sunday 13 September 2026 · Offence series, part 2*

---

## The main idea

**Offence is not first a problem between people. It is a problem inside us.**

A heart that has not been made new keeps pumping the same old reactions through us. So when offence comes, it is the offence that decides what comes out of us — not because offence is so strong, but because nothing inside has changed.

The answer is therefore not to try *harder* to react better.

The answer has two parts. **God must change what flows through us.** And then we have to learn how to live with a healthy heart. Growing into that new way of living is a lifelong process.

---

## Part one — the diagnosis

### The heart has one task

If offence were an illness in the body, it would be a heart condition.

The heart carries fresh blood to every part of the body, so that the body stays alive and strong. **That is its function.**

And when a heart cannot do that task, nothing looks wrong from the outside. The result is a weakened body — and we usually blame everything except our hearts.

### An unchanged heart works the same way

It keeps circulating the same old reactions. The same old habits. The same old ways of thinking.

That is why offence decides how we respond — not because the offence itself is so strong, but because nothing in us has changed. **The blood has nothing new to carry.**

So the honest question is not *who hurt me?*

The honest question is: **what is pumping through my heart?**

### Key verse — Ezekiel 36:26

> **Ezekiel 36:26 — ESV**
> And I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you. And I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh.
>
> **NLT**
> And I will give you a new heart, and I will put a new spirit in you. I will take out your stony, stubborn heart and give you a tender, responsive heart.

Look carefully at what God does **not** say here.

- He does not offer to repair the stone heart.
- He does not offer to teach it to behave better.
- He takes it **out** and gives a new one.

This is not a repair job. It is a **heart transplant**.

---

## The turn — the doctor and the therapist

**Jesus is not only the doctor who transplants the new heart and brings about our healing. He is also the therapist who teaches us how to live with that new heart.**

The doctor does the transplant. That work is finished, and He did it without our help.

The therapist handles everything that comes after.

### Why that difference matters

A person can be genuinely healed and still walk with a limp — never fully grasping or realising what the healing actually did.

Offence in a believer usually works the same way. It is **not** a heart that was never healed. It is a new heart still running the old heart's habits, because nobody ever showed it another way.

**The operation costs us nothing. The recovery costs us something every day.**

What follows are three things we lay down so that the new heart can do its work. An offering is something you bring and set down. Here are three.

---

## Part two — three things we lay down

### 1. Humility — I lay down my position

Humility is not thinking poorly of yourself or putting yourself down. **It is choosing to stand *under* something.**

It happens in three directions.

#### Under God — before whom I bow

> *"God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble. Submit yourselves therefore to God"* — James 4:6-7

God gives grace to the humble. But read it carefully: **humility is not the price I pay to buy grace. It is the posture in which I can receive it.**

Think of a cup under a tap. The cup has to be open, and it has to be underneath. It does not earn the water — it is simply in the right position to catch it.

#### Under authority — where I stand

Romans 13:1-2 says every authority is where it is because God placed it there.

This is the difficult one. It is also where most offence inside a church actually sits.

**Very few people take offence at God. Many people take offence at someone God has placed over them.**

And here is why it is so hard to see: **offence towards a leader almost never calls itself offence.**

We do not say *"I took offence."* We say:

- *"I have reservations."*
- *"I don't think that was handled well."*
- *"I just sense something."*

**Offence borrows the language of spiritual discernment** — so that nobody has to call it by its name. Ourselves included.

It is worth saying that plainly, rather than letting it sit quietly in the room.

#### Rooted — what I live from

A root is humble in two ways.

- It grows **down**, into the dark, where nobody sees it.
- And it admits that it **cannot make its own water**.

### Key verse — Psalm 1:3

> **Psalm 1:3 — ESV**
> He is like a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in its season, and its leaf does not wither. In all that he does, he prospers.
>
> **NLT**
> They are like trees planted along the riverbank, bearing fruit each season. Their leaves never wither, and they prosper in all they do.

A tree standing beside a stream you can see — that is easy to picture.

Jeremiah draws the same tree again, and adds the part you **cannot** see.

> **Jeremiah 17:8 — ESV**
> He is like a tree planted by water, that **sends out its roots by the stream**, and does not fear when heat comes, for its leaves remain green, and is not anxious in the year of drought, for it does not cease to bear fruit.

The roots go down. They push through the soil until they reach water.

**That is why a year of drought does not show on the leaves.**

A tree cannot make water, but it can go looking for it — and the roots do the digging.

*Strome van Lewe* — streams of life. The streams were there long before the church carried the name. **Our responsibility as a body is to go looking for the water, to reach out towards those streams of life.**

### And then Jesus sketches it a third time

> **John 15:5 — ESV**
> "I am the vine; you are the branches. Whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing."

> ### Blood. Water. Sap.
>
> Three pictures pointing to the same thing:
>
> ***Another source of Life — outside myself — that flows through me, and that I have to stay connected to if I want to keep receiving it.***

Cut a branch off the vine and it does not wilt that day. It stays green for a while.

**That is the danger: the evidence arrives late.** You do not notice it on the day the connection breaks. You notice it in the season when there is no fruit to pick.

> **So what do I lay down here?**
> My position. My right to be on top, to be the best — and with it, the idea that I can be self-sustaining.

---

### 2. Understanding the fight — I lay down my self-defence

**Truly knowing and understanding God in the middle of the fight is worth more than simply having factual knowledge about Him.**

> **Ephesians 6:10-11 — ESV**
> Finally, be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might. Put on the whole armour of God, that you may be able to stand against the schemes of the devil.
>
> **NLT**
> A final word: Be strong in the Lord and in his mighty power. Put on all of God's armour so that you will be able to stand firm against all strategies of the devil.

The exchange looks like this:

> **"When I know God in His love and take on His battle strategy, I will protect what He fights for."**

### Why that makes the difference

Someone who does not know what God fights for will defend whatever feels threatened. **Usually we defend ourselves.**

Knowing Him teaches me what is actually worth protecting. And in most cases, when I take offence, I am not protecting anything on God's list.

Look at the two lists side by side.

| What God fights for | What I fight for when I have taken offence |
|---|---|
| Reconciliation | My name |
| The lost | My version of the story |
| The unity of His body | The right to be right |
| His Name | My comfort |
| The truth — even when it counts against me | That the other person must admit they were wrong |

**They rarely overlap.**

### And here is where fear comes in

Behind a great deal of offence is not anger, but **fear**. This verse explains how.

> **1 John 4:18 — ESV**
> There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear. For fear has to do with punishment, and whoever fears has not been perfected in love.

Follow the chain slowly.

> **I am afraid of being punished.**
> So I cannot afford to be wrong.
>
> **Being wrong becomes dangerous.**
> Every disagreement turns into a threat, and I have to win it.
>
> **And if I cannot win?**
> Then the next best thing is to be the one who got hurt — because the one who got hurt is never the one who was wrong.

That is why offence is such a comfortable place to stand. **It is the safest seat in any argument.**

But perfect love takes the punishment off the table. And a person who is not facing punishment **can afford to be wrong**.

That is the freedom. I am no longer fighting to be right — and so I am free to fight for what God fights for.

> **So what do I lay down here?**
> My self-defence. The right to look after myself first.

---

### 3. The victory — I lay down my grip

> **1 John 5:4 — ESV**
> For everyone who has been born of God overcomes the world. And this is the victory that has overcome the world — our faith.
>
> **NLT**
> For every child of God defeats this evil world, and we achieve this victory through our faith.

So the victory is not a feeling. It is not a performance either.

**It is faith.** And faith is the one thing a hurt person can still do when there is nothing else left to do.

## Three things sit inside that victory

**Offence asks three questions. The victory answers all three.**

### 1. *"Am I still worth anything?"*

**You are God's first prize.**

We usually speak as though God is the prize we are working towards. Titus 2:14 says the opposite: Christ gave Himself *"to purify for himself a people for his own possession"*.

He did not come for what you can do. **Jesus came for *you*.**

**What this means in your offence:** the person who hurt you does not determine your worth — and cannot take it away. They never handed it out in the first place.

### 2. *"If I let go, won't everything fall apart?"*

**The faithfulness is His, not mine.**

> *"If we are faithless, he remains faithful — for he cannot deny himself"* — 2 Timothy 2:13

It is easy to hear this the wrong way round. It does **not** say I must hold on tighter. It says the holding was never mine.

That is why **nothing** — *"neither death nor life, nor angels nor rulers, nor things present nor things to come"* — can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:38-39).

**What this means in your offence:** no, it does not fall apart. His faithfulness holds it together. You can loosen your grip without everything collapsing.

### 3. *"Then what am I standing on?"*

**On Him.**

> *"In him we live and move and have our being"* — Acts 17:28

This is not a prize waiting at the end. **It is the ground I have been standing on the whole time.**

**What this means in your offence:** the ground under you does not shift when someone withdraws their approval. It was never their approval holding you up.

> **So what do I lay down here?**
> My grip. The quiet conviction that *I* am the one holding it all together.

---

## How it all connects to offence

Offence feeds on three things:

- a heart that has not been changed
- the fear of being punished if I let go
- the conviction that I am the one holding the relationship together

**And the three offerings are the exact antidote to what offence feeds on.**

| What we lay down | What it takes away |
|---|---|
| **Humility** | *The unchanged heart:* I stand under God, and I live from something outside myself. |
| **Knowing and trusting God in the fight** | *The fear:* perfect love means I can afford to be wrong. |
| **The victory** | *My grip:* His faithfulness holds it, not mine. |

**Take all three away, and offence has very little left to live on.**

## Application

### From the Word — Naaman (2 Kings 5:10-14)

Naaman was the commander of the Syrian army. Powerful, respected — and a leper.

A captive Israelite servant girl in his house told his wife about a prophet in Israel who could heal him. Naaman travelled there with horses, chariots and a small fortune in gifts.

The prophet Elisha did not even come to the door. He sent out a messenger with an instruction: **go and wash yourself seven times in the Jordan River.**

Naaman rode off furious. Listen to his reason:

> *"Behold, I thought that he would surely come out to me and stand and call upon the name of the Lord his God, and wave his hand over the place and cure the leper."* — verse 11

**He did not take offence at the healing. He took offence at the way it came.**

- He was handled through a messenger instead of being met personally.
- And he was sent to a muddy little river when there were better rivers back home.

It took his own officials to say the obvious thing:

> *"My father, it is a great word the prophet has spoken to you; will you not do it? Has he actually said to you, 'Wash, and be clean'?"* — verse 13

If the prophet had asked something **difficult** of him, he would gladly have done it.

The instruction was too small to be worth his dignity. And it nearly cost him his skin.

**This is offence at the medicine, not at the doctor.**

It happens in churches far more than we like to admit. Very few people refuse the healing. Many people refuse the **form** it comes in:

- **through a person they did not choose**
- **in a way that feels beneath them** — too small, too simple, not worthy of their standing
- **in a way that exposes them** — where others can see they needed help

### The transplanted heart

A heart transplant does not end at the operation. **The new heart is the easier part.** Everything that follows is the therapist's work — and here is exactly why it is needed.

Your own immune system has protected you your whole life. But it does not recognise the new heart. It sees an **intruder**, and it attacks it.

That is why a heart patient needs a lifetime of medication. Not because the operation failed — the operation was a success. But because the body itself turns against the healing, **and it does not stop on its own.**

You have to be taught how to live healthy. Every day, deliberately.

### The reaction everyone can predict

Ask someone who has known you a long time. A husband or wife. A brother or sister. Someone you have worked beside for years.

Ask them what you do when someone opposes you.

**They will not need time to think.** They will tell you straight away, and they will be right.

That answer is what your heart has been pumping all these years.

It is also the only honest place to start. **A heart condition is not diagnosed by how we feel about ourselves. It is diagnosed by what comes out under pressure.**

## Questions

### Personal

1. Ask someone who knows you well what you do when someone opposes you. Don't defend the answer — write it down. **What has your heart been pumping all this time?**
2. Where have you taken offence at the *way* something came to you, rather than at the thing itself? The person who said it, the tone, the place, the fact that nobody asked you first?
3. What are you still holding onto as evidence — in case things go wrong and you need to show it wasn't your fault?
4. Of the three things we lay down — my position, my self-defence, my grip — **which one is God asking of you this week?**

### For the group

1. Humility is described in three directions: under God, under authority, rooted. Which one is easiest to talk about in a group like ours — and which one do we quietly skip?
2. Offence towards a leader almost never calls itself offence. How would we recognise the difference in **ourselves** — not in someone else?
3. The roots go looking for water *before* the dry year comes, not during it. What does "going looking" look like in an ordinary week?
4. If the faithfulness holding things together is His and not ours — what does that change about how we handle a relationship that has gone quiet?

---

## Prayer focus

That we would stop treating the **reaction**, and let God work on the **heart**.

And that we would be people whose roots went looking for water long before the dry year — so that when the heat comes, it does not show on our leaves.
